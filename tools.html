How CBAM Works

Four steps from export to
certificate obligation.

01
Identify covered goods
Determine whether your HS codes fall within CBAM's six regulated sectors: steel, iron, aluminium, cement, fertilisers, hydrogen, and electricity.
02
Calculate embedded emissions
Report total CO₂ and N₂O embedded in the goods — direct (Scope 1) and in some sectors indirect (Scope 2). Until you have verified data, use EU default values.
03
Deduct carbon costs paid
Any carbon price paid in the country of production (India, GCC, etc.) is deductible from CBAM liability — provided it can be evidenced to EU Customs.
04
Purchase CBAM certificates
EU declarants (your EU importer) submit CBAM declarations and surrender certificates annually. Price tracks weekly EU ETS auctions.
Regulated Sectors
Six sectors face CBAM from 2026 onwards.

India and GCC are among the top exporters in steel, aluminium, and fertilisers to the EU. Exporters in these sectors are most immediately affected.
